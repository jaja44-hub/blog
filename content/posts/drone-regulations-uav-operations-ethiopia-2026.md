# Drone Regulations and Commercial UAV Operations in Ethiopia: Legal Guide (2026)

## Executive Summary

🔴 **Regulatory Compliance in Commercial UAV Operations**: Operating Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) or drones in Ethiopia for commercial aerial photography, agricultural survey, infrastructure inspection, mapping, or logistics requires navigating a multi-agency regulatory ecosystem. Governed primarily by the **Ethiopian Civil Aviation Authority (ECAA)** under **Civil Aviation Proclamation No. 1068/2017** and strict national security oversight by the **Information Network Security Administration (INSA)**, unapproved drone operations are illegal. Drones are classified under national security laws as dual-use surveillance technologies; unauthorized import, assembly, or flight operations trigger severe criminal liabilities, equipment confiscation, and administrative penalties.

🎯 **Key Takeaways for Commercial Operators & Businesses**:
- **Dual Regulatory Approval**: Operating commercial drones requires dual permits: security clearance and frequency allocation from **INSA**, followed by operational airworthiness certification and flight permits from the **ECAA**.
- **Mandatory UAV Registration**: Under **ECAA Unmanned Aircraft System (UAS) Directives**, all drones weighing over 250 grams must be formally registered, marked with a unique national registration number, and issued a Certificate of Airworthiness.
- **Flight Altitude & Airspace Restrictions**: Commercial drone flights are strictly limited to Visual Line of Sight (VLOS) operations below **400 feet (120 meters) Above Ground Level (AGL)**, with absolute no-fly zones within 10 kilometers of airports, military installations, and government complexes.
- **Import Permit Requirements**: Importing UAV hardware requires pre-shipment security import permits from INSA and customs release authorization from the **Ethiopian Customs Commission (ECC)**.

💡 **Target Audience**: This comprehensive legal guide provides practical compliance strategies for commercial drone operators, media production companies, agricultural tech firms, infrastructure surveyors, logistics startups, and legal advisors in 2026.

## Legal Framework Overview

⚖️ **Governing Legislation and Statutory Mandates**: Unmanned aircraft systems, commercial aerial photography, radio frequency utilization, and airspace security in Ethiopia are regulated by primary federal proclamations and agency directives:

1. **Civil Aviation Proclamation No. 1068/2017**: The primary aviation statute granting the Ethiopian Civil Aviation Authority (ECAA) exclusive jurisdiction over airspace management, aircraft safety standards, pilot certification, and flight authorizations.
2. **Information Network Security Administration (INSA) Establishment Proclamation No. 808/2013**: Grants INSA statutory authority over national cryptographic security, electronic surveillance hardware, radio frequency allocation, and dual-use technology import clearances.
3. **ECAA Unmanned Aircraft System (UAS) Operational Directive (Directive No. 02/2024)**: Regulates UAV weight classifications, airworthiness registration, Remote Pilot License (RPL) requirements, and operational flight rules.
4. **Customs Proclamation No. 859/2014 (Amended by Proclamation No. 1160/2019)**: Regulates customs clearance, import duty tariffs, and restricted dual-use technology confiscations.
5. **Telecom Fraud Offences Proclamation No. 761/2012**: Penalizes the unauthorized deployment of radio frequency transmission equipment and encrypted signal broadcast hardware.

Key statutory provisions governing drone operations include:
- **Proclamation 1068/2017, Article 18**: Granting ECAA sole regulatory authority over all civil aircraft operating within Ethiopian airspace, explicitly including unmanned aerial vehicles.
- **Proclamation 808/2013, Article 6**: Mandating INSA security vetting and pre-import clearance for all electronic surveillance, aerial mapping, and transmission devices.
- **Directive 02/2024, Section 4**: Categorizing UAVs by weight: Category 1 (Micro: < 250g), Category 2 (Small: 250g – 25kg), Category 3 (Medium: 25kg – 150kg), and Category 4 (Heavy: > 150kg).
- **Directive 02/2024, Section 12**: Mandatory third-party liability insurance coverage for all commercial UAV operations.

🏛️ **Regulatory Authorities and Institutional Oversight**:
1. **Ethiopian Civil Aviation Authority (ECAA)**: Regulates civil airspace, evaluates UAV airworthiness, issues Remote Pilot Licenses (RPL), approves flight corridors, and coordinates Notice to Airmen (NOTAM) issuances.
2. **Information Network Security Administration (INSA)**: Conducts security background checks on drone operators, inspects onboard sensors/cameras, allocates transmission frequencies, and issues pre-shipment import authorizations.
3. **Ethiopian Customs Commission (ECC)**: Enforces border controls to intercept unapproved drone imports at airports and land customs posts.
4. **Ethiopian Communications Authority (ECA)**: Regulates radio frequency spectrum usage for ground control stations and video downlink telemetry.

⏱️ **Recent Regulatory and Security Updates**:
In 2026, ECAA and INSA streamlined commercial drone permitting by establishing an integrated digital portal for flight clearance requests. The updated framework introduces temporary flight permits for international film crews and specialized agricultural surveying companies, while enforcing strict geofencing firmware requirements for all drones operating within urban centers.

### Step 1: UAV Classification and Use-Case Assessment
⏱️ **Timeline**: 1–2 Days | 💰 **Cost**: Internal Assessment | ⚖️ **Governing Provision**: ECAA Directive No. 02/2024 Section 4

🔴 **Critical Requirement**: Drones must be properly categorized based on maximum takeoff weight (MTOW) and operational intent before initiating regulatory permit applications.
- **Weight Categories**:
  - **Category 1 (Micro < 250g)**: Educational/hobbyist use; exempt from formal registration unless equipped with high-resolution thermal or mapping sensors.
  - **Category 2 (Small 250g – 25kg)**: Standard commercial photography, agricultural inspection, and mapping drones; mandatory registration and flight permits required.
  - **Category 3 (Medium 25kg – 150kg)**: Industrial spraying and heavy cargo drones; requires full airworthiness inspection and certified pilots.
  - **Category 4 (Heavy > 150kg)**: Large industrial/cargo UAVs governed by commercial aircraft airworthiness standards.

---

### Step 2: INSA Pre-Import Security Clearance and Frequency Allocation
⏱️ **Timeline**: 5–10 Business Days | 💰 **Cost**: ~2,000–5,000 ETB Application Fee | ⚖️ **Governing Provision**: Proclamation 808/2013 Article 6

🔴 **Critical Requirement**: Importing drone hardware into Ethiopia without prior INSA clearance results in immediate seizure at customs and potential criminal investigation under telecom fraud laws.
- **Application Procedure**: Submit technical specifications to INSA, including drone model, serial numbers, camera resolution, thermal sensor specs, operational frequency band (e.g., 2.4 GHz / 5.8 GHz), and ground control software details.
- **Outcome**: INSA issues a **Pre-Import Security Clearance Certificate** and radio frequency authorization.

---

### Step 3: Customs Clearance and Border Release
⏱️ **Timeline**: 2–4 Business Days | 💰 **Cost**: Import Duty (0%–35%) + 15% VAT + 3% Advance Income Tax | ⚖️ **Governing Provision**: Customs Proclamation No. 859/2014

✅ **Best Practice**: Present the original INSA clearance certificate to Ethiopian Customs Commission officers at Bole International Airport or land customs points to prevent hardware impoundment.
- **Duty Assessment**: Commercial survey and agricultural drones imported under capital investment permits may qualify for duty-free privileges under **Investment Regulation No. 474/2020**.

---

### Step 4: ECAA UAV Airworthiness Registration and Marking
⏱️ **Timeline**: 3–5 Business Days | 💰 **Cost**: ~3,000–8,000 ETB Registration Fee | ⚖️ **Governing Provision**: ECAA Directive No. 02/2024 Section 6

🟡 **Caution**: Operating an unregistered commercial drone in Ethiopian airspace is a federal aviation offense.
- **Procedure**: Present the hardware to ECAA technical inspectors for physical inspection, fail-safe testing (return-to-home functionality), and serial number validation.
- **Outcome**: ECAA issues a **UAV Certificate of Registration** and assigns a unique national registration mark (e.g., **ET-UAV-XXXX**) that must be permanently affixed to the drone frame.

---

### Step 5: Remote Pilot Licensing (RPL) and Qualification
⏱️ **Timeline**: 5–10 Business Days | 💰 **Cost**: ~4,000–10,000 ETB Examination & Licensing Fee | ⚖️ **Governing Provision**: ECAA Directive No. 02/2024 Section 8

📊 **Pilot Certification Requirements**:
- Must be at least **18 years of age**.
- Must pass an ECAA medical fitness examination.
- Must complete ground school training covering airspace classification, meteorology, air law, and emergency protocols.
- Must pass a practical flight test evaluated by an ECAA flight examiner.
- Foreign pilots must validate their foreign Remote Pilot Certificate with ECAA.

---

### Step 6: Flight Permit Approval, Coordinates Clearance, and NOTAM
⏱️ **Timeline**: 3–5 Business Days Prior to Flight | 💰 **Cost**: ~2,000–5,000 ETB Permit Fee | ⚖️ **Governing Provision**: Proclamation 1068/2017 & ECAA Rules

🔴 **Critical Requirement**: Every commercial drone flight mission requires a specific location and time-bound flight authorization.
- **Procedure**: Submit GPS bounding box coordinates, proposed flight altitude, flight duration, and purpose to ECAA and INSA.
- **Security Supervision**: INSA may assign a security liaison officer to oversee sensitive aerial mapping or infrastructure survey flights.
- **NOTAM Issuance**: For operations near controlled airspace, ECAA issues a Notice to Airmen informing regional aircraft of unmanned flight activity.

---

### Step 7: Flight Execution, Insurance, and Post-Mission Log Reporting
⏱️ **Timeline**: Operational Phase | 💰 **Cost**: Mandatory Liability Insurance Premium | ⚖️ **Governing Provision**: ECAA Directive No. 02/2024 Section 12

✅ **Best Practice**: Always maintain flight logs detailing takeoff times, landing times, GPS tracks, battery cycles, and pilot details.
- **Insurance Requirement**: Commercial operators must hold active third-party liability insurance covering damage to property and persons.
- **Post-Flight Reporting**: Submit completed flight logs and sensor recording reports to ECAA upon request.
