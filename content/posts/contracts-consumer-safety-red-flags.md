## Introduction

Everyday transactions – from gym memberships to software subscriptions to home repair services – come with contracts most people sign without reading. In Ethiopia's growing market economy, understanding what you're agreeing to is increasingly important.

## Red Flag 1: Unlimited Liability Clauses

Some contracts contain clauses that make you liable for "all direct, indirect, consequential, or incidental damages." This can expose you to financial ruin far beyond the original transaction value. **What to look for:** Language that shifts risk entirely to one party without balance.

## Red Flag 2: Automatic Renewal Traps

Subscription services often bury automatic renewal terms in fine print. A clause may renew your subscription indefinitely unless you cancel 30 days before the term ends – in language that's easy to miss. **Pro tip:** Circle every "automatic," "renew," or "continuation" phrase.

## Red Flag 3: Arbitration-Only Dispute Resolution

Many contracts require you to waive your right to sue in the court and instead use binding arbitration. While arbitration can be faster, it can also be biased toward the company, and you lose the ability to set precedent. **Question to ask:** "Can I ever take this to court, and under what circumstances?"

## Red Flag 4: Choice-of-Law Provisions

A contract may specify that Ethiopian law doesn't apply, even if you're a Ethiopian consumer. This can leave you without familiar legal protections. **Red flag:** Choice-of-law clauses that select jurisdictions with consumer protections far below Ethiopia's standards.

## Red Flag 5: Unilateral Modification Rights

Some contracts give the company power to change terms at any time, with continued use deemed acceptance. This means your agreement can change without your active consent. **What you can do:** Negotiate a clause requiring 30-day written notice before any material change.

## How to Protect Yourself

1. **Read before you sign** – Even 15 minutes of review can catch major issues.
2. **Ask for a copy before signing** – You should be able to review the full contract.
3. **Highlight unclear terms** – Don't sign anything you don't understand.
4. **Consult a professional** – For contracts involving significant money or rights, 200 birr for a quick review can save thousands.
5. **Negotiate** – Standard form contracts are often negotiable, especially for repeat business.

## Conclusion

Consumer contracts aren't inherently bad – they establish clear expectations. But knowing how to read them gives you the power to protect your rights and avoid surprises. If a term seems questionable, it probably is. Trust your instincts and seek clarification before committing.
